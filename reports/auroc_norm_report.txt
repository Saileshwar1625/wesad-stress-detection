How to read this:
  - Flagged subject with HIGH AUROC and a large gap, or mean_p_stress far from prevalence: the model ranks that person's windows well but the scores sit on the wrong side of the cutoff. That is a threshold/shift failure, not a failure to separate stress from non-stress.
  - Flagged subject with LOW AUROC, still low after normalization: the model cannot separate that person's stress windows from non-stress ones at all (ranking failure).
  - Tree models (rf, gb) can saturate: if a subject's features fall outside the range seen in training, every window lands in the same leaves and the scores tie, so AUROC drops toward 0.5 and the subject is labelled 'ranking' even though the cause is a between-subject offset. The tell is that normalization restores AUROC. Linear models (lr) shift smoothly, so there the gap shows up as a threshold failure instead.
  - 'none' vs 'full' vs 'earlyN': if scores improve with 'full' but not with 'earlyN', the gain depends on knowing the whole recording and would not be available in deployment. 'earlyN' is only meaningful because every WESAD recording starts at rest.
  - bal_acc_best_threshold uses the subject's own labels; it shows what a perfect per-person cutoff could achieve, not what a deployed model gets.
  - ~35 independent windows per subject: single-subject differences under ~0.1 are noise.
